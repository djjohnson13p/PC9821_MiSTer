# Independent review: the RMW guard and the gated-clock throttle (2026-10-10)

Scope: two unpushed change sets on `personal/main` `2c1426c`.

- `rmw-guard`: `cd732a7` (`tests:` sim-only assertion) and `80e41b4` (`fix(z486)`, one OR-term
  in `mem_op_eligible`).
- `throttle-gated`: the CPU-speed throttle redesigned as "stop the whole CPU" with a gated clock
  (mode A). Seven commits: `54b4595`, `20175cd`, `4a75479`, `57aee14`, `029ffc5`, `d8f39f9` and
  `8ab5523`.

They are integrated, folded per `docs/fork-rebase-policy.md` and corrected on branch
`cpu-review2`. The source branches are untouched.

**Verdict: ready to push `cpu-review2`, with conditions.** It is ready as a fork series. The
throttle is NOT ready to ship in the PC-9821 core until that core's integration items below are
done and measured. The fork series is safe for the core to re-vendor first, with the selector
left at full speed, *provided* the core makes the mechanical port changes (section 6, items 1
and 10). At setting 0 the series is cycle-identical to `2c1426c` (section 4).

## 3. Verdicts per commit

### 1. tests: sim-only guard (`9f1bceb`): OK

This is a `translate_off` `$fatal` on `i_issue && d2_vipt_rmw && mem_req_to_paging && mem_accepted`.
It is synthesis-neutral (the `quartus_map` below is on the integrated head). It never fired: not
in the suite at latencies 0, 7 and 15, not in `test-release`, and not in the fuzzers below.

### 2. fix(z486) RMW guard (`dade7fe`): OK, cycle-neutral, with a recorded structural assumption

**Cycle-neutral, confirmed independently.** The full protected suite was run at latencies 0, 7
and 15 on `2c1426c` and on the integrated head: 975 runs, identical verdict, cycles and
instructions (section 4). Every microbenchmark of the previous review and `hz_rmw_late_finish`
are identical at latencies 0, 5 and 15. Dhrystone is unchanged.

**Can the term deadlock a bus op?** The term lowers `mem_op_eligible` only when the predecessor
is in its RNI delay slot (`i_rni_delay`) while D2 holds an RMW candidate. If that delay-slot word
carried a microcode bus op, `mem_block_idle` would stall the slot. The RMW cannot issue under a
stall (`d2_ready_base` needs `!stall`), and `d2_vipt_rmw_candidate` is a pure function of the
decoded instruction and mode. **Nothing would break that wait.** This is exactly the structure of
the load term that has been there since `5f8ba7b`
(`!(i_rni_delay && d2_vipt_candidate)`), so the RMW term inherits the load term's assumption: a
microcode bus op never sits in an RNI delay slot while D2 holds a direct candidate.

The review measured that assumption, using a monitor build that is not committed. It counted
cycles with `i_rni_delay && uc_active && uc_busreq`, and the same with a resident load or RMW
candidate. The RMW case was a `$fatal`.

- The suite was run at latencies 0, 7 and 32 (978 runs). Delay-slot bus ops do occur: 38 runs
  across 16 programs, including `hw_interrupt`, `intr_pop_esp`, `v86_int_stack_reuse` and
  `wbinvd_flush_rm`. The monitor recorded **0 with a load candidate and 0 with an RMW
  candidate**.
- `fuzz_hazard --rmw` (40 seeds), `fuzz_hazard` (30) and `fuzz_hazard_emm` (20) all match, so
  the `$fatal` never fired.

So the window is unreached, the term never acts, and that is why it is cycle-neutral. There is no
deadlock in anything exercised.

Recommendation (not blocking): turn the assumption into a sim fuse. A `translate_off` `$fatal`
when a delay-slot bus op is held off by either D2 term would make a future microcode change that
breaks it fail loudly rather than hang. Both terms rely on it.

### 3. pc98(throttle): gate the CPU clock, delete the park (`b2370f1`): OK after R1

**Park deletion.** Every removed term was constant at setting 0, so removing it is a constant
fold at full speed.

- `throttle_hold` is a register reset to 0, forced to 0 when `full_speed`, and cleared on any
  `speed_sel` change.
- `throttle_parked_r` is set only under `throttle_hold`.
- `throttle_release_cycle` is gated by `throttle_parked_r`.

The removed terms are:

- `d2_ready_base`'s `(!throttle_hold || atomic_chain || release_cycle)`
- `uc_slot_live`'s `!throttle_parked_r`
- `mem_op_eligible`'s `!throttle_parked_r`
- `hardwired_control`'s `pb_b1_ready` `!throttle_hold`
- the event-control fuse and the park register

Measured: identical per-program cycles and Dhrystone (section 4).

**Gated clock is architecturally invisible inside z486.**

- Every flop and RAM in z486 is on `clk`.
- The asynchronous resets (`decoder`, `paging_tlb`, `paging_unit`, `dsp_mul` and `z486.sv`'s
  `early_redirected`) still work, and they are not clock-dependent.
- No logic counts real time.
- The only inputs whose meaning depends on being seen at one particular edge are the pulse
  inputs. They are `resp_valid`, `line_resp_valid` (and the data with them), `snoop_valid`/
  `snoop_addr`, `nmi` (edge-detected in `interrupt_controller.sv`), and `ready` and
  `cache_flush` wherever the platform pulses them.

The contract now lists all of them (R5).

**Enable generator.** Its rate is exact and independent of `clk_sys`. A standalone unit bench
(not committed; `.build/z486-review2-logs/thr-unit/`) counted `cpu_ce` over `2 x CLOCK_RATE_KHZ`
cycles for every code at `CLOCK_RATE_KHZ` = 50000, 80000, 85000 and 93750.

- Every code gives **exactly** `2 x rate` enables, so the edge rate is `rate_khz` kHz at every
  platform clock.
- The longest wait is at most `ceil(CLOCK_RATE_KHZ/rate)`. At 93.75 MHz and 0.5 MHz it is 188.
- 0, 14 and 15 give a constant 1 (after R1).
- `cpu_ce` is 1 throughout reset.

Width: `AW = clog2(K+1)+1` holds `acc + inc < 2K`, because `inc < K` whenever the accumulator is
live. A selector change takes effect the next cycle with no transient. Setting 0 is a strict
no-op for the CPU's own logic (`cpu_ce` is constant 1). On hardware the gated clock still goes
through the ALTCLKCTRL (section 5).

**Testbench.** `tb_protected_mode` puts the memory model, the 0xFC clock and accept detection on
the ungated clock. Observers and interrupt placement run on `cclk`. Responses go through
`cpu_pulse_hold` with back-pressure, and the latency countdown pauses while a beat waits. The
bench's `ready` is registered, so the CPU and the bench see the same handshake at every enabled
edge. The `xdma` snoop is a single pulse, so its unconnected `snoop_stall` is acceptable here.

### 5. tests: throttle targets, invariance, fuzzers (`fd5ac52`): OK after R2

Two skipped programs, reasoned through:

- **`rep_stos_intr`: legitimately skipped.** It arms a cycle-delay INTR (signal port,
  `signal_delay_cycles` counted in CPU edges). The REP iteration it lands on depends on how many
  CPU cycles each store's memory latency costs, and that shrinks as the CPU slows against the
  bus. The handler stores `intr_ecx`/`intr_edi` to memory, so the final-memory hash differs
  legitimately, while the verdict (any mid-REP landing passes) does not. `test-protected-throttled`
  still checks its verdict at every setting.
- **`hz_rmw_late_finish`: wrongly skipped** (R2). Its `out 0xe8` writes 0x77, which is inert, and
  it runs with `cli`. Nothing in it depends on the setting, and it now matches at every setting
  and latency.

**Comparison artifact in the volume sweep.** The `inv3` run compares every
(setting, latency) against full speed at latency **1**. Its two `lock_fault_run` "mismatches"
(setting 1 and 3 at latency 32) are latency failures, not throttle failures. `lock_fault_run`
carries `+max_lock_run=60`, in CPU cycles, and fails at latency 32 *at full speed* on `2c1426c`
too. So do `lock_run_ctl`, `lock_ud` and `intr_after_lock`, and `vipt_rmw_interval` fails at
latencies 7 and 15. All of these are pre-existing and identical at base and head. It passes at
slower settings because 32 platform cycles are fewer CPU cycles there.

`test-release` uses latencies 1 and 9, so it is unaffected. Suggested follow-up: compare against
a full-speed reference at the SAME latency, and skip a latency whose reference fails, so the
sweep isolates the throttle.

**What only the PC-9821 integration can measure:**

- the real fit's `Total LABs` with the platform-side pieces added (the `cpu_throttle` generator,

## 7. PC-9821 core integration checklist

This checklist was derived from the core's RTL at `91db3cd1`. The author's own list was not
available to this review (it was not in either tree), so diff this list against it.

1. **Port and parameter removal**, mechanical and required at re-vendor even at full speed.
   - `src/pc9821/core/pc9821_system.sv`: the z486 instance loses `.CLOCK_RATE_MHZ(CPU_CLOCK_MHZ)`
     and `.cpu_speed_sel(cpu_speed_sel)`.
   - `tests/pc9821/z486_stub.sv` loses its `CLOCK_RATE_MHZ` parameter and `cpu_speed_sel [3:0]`
     port.
   - `tests/pc9821/tb_pc9821_gvram_pagecopy.sv` and `tb_pc9821_grcg_cpu.sv` drop
     `.cpu_speed_sel(4'd0)`.
   - `tools/check_port_lists.py` will flag any of these that are missed.
2. **The rate generator in the platform.** Add `cpu_throttle #(.CLOCK_RATE_KHZ(CLOCK_RATE_HZ /
   1000))` in `pc9821_system` (93.75 MHz -> 93750 kHz is exact).
   - Use `reset_n = cpu_reset_n`. `pc9821_cpu_reset_gate`'s reset is far longer than the
     2-cycle minimum.
   - Widen `pc9821_system`'s `cpu_speed_sel` port from `[1:0]` to `[3:0]`.
   - In `z486_mister.sv`, restore `status[17:14]` in place of `2'd0` and add one `CONF_STR` entry
     with the 14 plain-rate labels in table order (full, 33, 25, 16, 12, 8, 6, 4, 3, 2, 1.5, 1,
     0.75, 0.5 MHz). Run `check_osd_contract.py`.
   - Update todo item 83, the comment block in `z486_mister.sv` (about lines 419-428),
     `pc9821_system.sv`'s port comment ("1 = 15 MHz ..."), and `docs/z486-local-patch.md`'s
     throttle entry.
3. **The gated clock.** Add an `ALTCLKCTRL` with `inclk = clk_sys` and `ena = cpu_ce`, feeding
   z486's `clk` only. Do NOT synthesise `cpu_clk_gate`, which is a latch-AND for simulation.
   - Check every SDC line that names CPU registers through the `clk_sys` clock (the
     `general[0]` PLL output).
   - The clock-control block passes the clock through, but confirm the generated SDC's clock
     groups and multicycles still cover the CPU's registers.
4. **Bus handshake: one bridge between z486 and the platform.** This is the substantive work.
   - Today `z_ready`/`z_resp_valid` are a combinational mux of `mem_cpu_ready`, `io_cpu_ready`
     and `inta_ready` (`pc9821_system.sv` about lines 559-569), and the responders treat
     `z_valid && z_ready` as the transfer. At least these consumers do: the memory subsystem, I/O,
     INTA, `pc9821_wdg_trigger`, `pc9821_gfx_msg`'s `cpu_ack`, and the `m_*` port at about line
     2223.
   - Under the gate, `valid` stays high across non-enabled cycles, so a responder that answers in
     one of them would complete a transfer the CPU never saw, and then see `valid` again.
   - The bridge must:
     - present `valid` to the platform only while no accept is pending for the CPU;
     - hold the accept (`ready`) until the next `cpu_ce` cycle;
     - buffer `resp_valid` with `din`. The responders (SDRAM bursts, I/O) cannot be
       back-pressured, so this needs a **4-deep response FIFO**: `burstcount` is at most 4,
       `line_read` is unused and `line_resp_valid` is tied to 0.
   - `inta_ready` drives `ready` and `resp_valid` together, and both must reach the same CPU
     edge.
   - The bridge must be a pure wire when `cpu_ce` is 1, so the boot golden cannot move.
5. **Cache flush.** `pc9821_iobus.sv` drives `cache_flush_req = flush_start`, a one-cycle pulse,
   into z486's `cache_flush`, so it needs a hold. Its `flush_busy`/`flush_done` handshake
   (`pc9821_iobus.sv` about lines 602-604 and 665-670) must sample `cache_flush_done` under
   `cpu_ce`. A done several platform cycles wide must not complete or retrigger twice.
6. **`triple_fault_reset` -> `software_reset`** (`pc9821_system.sv` about line 2250): the pulse
   is widened. That is harmless if it only asserts reset, but gate it with `cpu_ce` if anything
   counts it or detects an edge on it.
7. **Debug and telemetry.** Gate everything that counts CPU progress with `cpu_ce`:
   - `dbg_issue`, `dbg_gate_read` and `dbg_page_fault`;
   - the trace ring (`TRACE_EN`) and the perf log;
   - the watchdog (`pc9821_wdg_trigger`'s `since_completion`). A throttled CPU otherwise looks
     stalled, so count stalls in CPU edges or scale the threshold.
8. **No change** is needed for:
   - `nmi`, `snoop_*` and `line_resp_valid` (all tied to 0);
   - `intr`, `a20_enable`, `win0_unmapped` and `ram_cache_top` (true levels);
   - `fast_off_req`, `cache_off_req` and `x87_off_req` (tied).
9. **Benches.** Keep `cpu_throttle.sv` in the bench lists; the platform now instantiates it.
   `gen_bench_sources.py` then `--check`. Add a platform bench that runs the CPU at a throttled
   setting through the bridge: a burst read, an I/O read, INTA, a cache flush and a reset.
10. **Gates** (AGENTS.md): run the full battery, `run_integrated_build.sh` (the port removal
    touches the integrated tops) and the z486 suite. Its count grows by this series' new
    programs, so re-pin and attribute it. Also run Dhrystone (unchanged, 254235) and the boot
    golden `CYCLES used=930530` at setting 0, which must not move.
