# Independent review: the INTR latch re-arm fix in interrupt_controller (2026-10-11)

Scope: branch `fix-irq-wedge`, two commits on `personal/main` `1b01c8f`.

| # | commit | subject |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `0b5b782` | tests: INTR re-requested at or just after its acknowledge (+intr_rerequest, +intr_regap, +intr_drop_delay) |
| 2 | `ebd1879` | fix(interrupt_controller): re-arm the INTR latch at the next instruction boundary, not only on INTR low |

**Verdict: PASS. Yes, push `fix-irq-wedge` as it stands.** The fix is correct against 486
behaviour, and `i_rni` is the right event: after CINTLA it fires only at the delivery's own final
microword. The PIC tolerance it relies on is real at full speed. My worst case is 44 platform
cycles, against a PIC that drops INTR in the cycle of the vector transfer or the next one. The
fail-first holds for the stated reason at full speed and at settings 7, 9 and 13. The change is
cycle-neutral, and the gates are green. I did not change the branch: both commits are exactly the
author's, so the core lane re-vendors `ebd1879` unchanged. The nits in section 6 are cosmetic, and
none of them is CHANGES-NEEDED.

## 1. The defect, reproduced

`0b5b782` is `1b01c8f`'s RTL plus the new bench. I built it and `ebd1879` in clean `git archive`
trees on a build host. I ran the eight new programs at `+cpu_speed` 0/7/9/13 x `+mem_latency` 0/7:

| program | `0b5b782` full speed | `0b5b782` at 7, 9 and 13 | `ebd1879`, every cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| `intr_rerequest_ack`, `pm_intr_rerequest_ack`, `pm_intr_rerequest_trap_ack` | FAIL | FAIL | PASS |
| `intr_rerequest_gap`, `pm_intr_rerequest_gap`, `pm_intr_rerequest_trap_gap` | PASS | FAIL | PASS |
| `hw_interrupt_slow_drop`, `pm_trap_gate_slow_drop` | PASS | PASS | PASS |

The failures are for the stated reason. Each failing run reports `TEST DATA 0x00010001`, which
means code 1 (timed out waiting for four entries) with `intr_count` = 1. `+trace_io` shows exactly
one `INTA cycle 2`, then none. On `ebd1879` the trace shows four INTA pairs, three of them
re-requested with a zero gap.

**The guards discriminate.** I built a mutant that re-arms the latch one cycle after CINTLA
(`if (1) intr_latch_inhibit <= 0`). Under it, both `*_slow_drop` guards time out, and so do plain
`hw_interrupt` and `pm_trap_gate_if_preserve`, because the bench's INTR is still high at CINTLA. A
re-arm that comes too early cannot pass.

## 2. Correctness

### 2.1 Is `i_rni` exactly the right event?

`i_rni` is `rni_base || jcc_fold_active || branch_ustep_rni || load_wb_retire`, gated in `z486.sv`
by `!rmw_fallback_delay_r`, so the RMW fallback replay is excluded. `rni_base` is a raw decode of
the resident word's opcode field (000/010, or 001 after a taken jump). It is the same signal that
already ends the STI/MOV SS shadow in this module.

- **No early boundary inside the delivery.** `830` (CINTLA) is the only CINTLA word
  (`ucode_disasm.py --find-aluop ALUJMP_CINTLA`). Words `82D`-`839` all have opcode 7 or 6, and
  nothing between CINTLA and the second IACK at `833` can raise `i_rni`.
- **What the simulator shows.** I put a sim-only monitor in a scratch copy of the bench (not
  committed). It records the microword and the platform-cycle distance of the first `i_rni` after
  `intr_latch_inhibit` rises. I ran it over every program at full speed, and over
  `fuzz_hazard_v86 --irq 300` and `fuzz_hazard_emm --irq 300` (16 seeds, 20,607 deliveries). The
  first `i_rni` was always one of the two delivery tails:
  - `8E3`, the same-privilege tail `USTEP_FAULT_DONE` / `DEST_CS`, at 40-126 platform cycles;
  - `639`, the inter-privilege / V86 tail `BUSOP_SDEL` `USTEP_FAULT_DONE` / `DEST_CS`, at 109-126
    platform cycles.

  So the re-arm lands on the first boundary after the delivery, never earlier. At `+cpu_speed=7`
  the monitor also logged `t=0` duplicates and two `9CF` (INC) hits. Those come from the monitor
  itself: it samples on every platform cycle while the CPU flops only move on enabled edges, so it
  re-armed on the still-set inhibit. They are not CPU behaviour.
- **Never later than intended.** `i_rni` is a level held while the word stalls, and clearing the
  inhibit is idempotent. A stalled or faulting tail word therefore costs at most an early clear
  *after* the second INTA, which is harmless.

### 2.2 Does it ever take an interrupt a 486 would not?

A 486 has no latch inhibit at all. It samples INTR at each boundary with IF=1. The new rule masks
INTR from CINTLA through the end of the delivery, then behaves like a 486. It is therefore never
more permissive than the 486, and strictly less permissive than the old rule only where the old
rule was the bug. I checked each case:

| case | with the fix |
|---|---|
| INTR still asserted for the request just acknowledged | It is masked until the delivery ends. It is latched only if it is still high at the delivery's end with IF=1. A real 8259 has dropped INT by the end of the second INTA. |
| real mode, interrupt gate | IF is already 0 at the tail, so nothing can latch until IRET or STI. Cycle tables are identical (section 3). |
| trap gate (IF stays 1) | A still-asserted INTR latches at the tail and is taken at the end of the handler's first instruction. That is one instruction later than a 486 could, never earlier. `pm_intr_rerequest_trap_*` pins the nesting. |
| task gate | IF comes from the new TSS. The tail is a long task switch, so the margin is larger than for a trap gate. |
| STI / MOV SS / POP SS shadow | `inhibit_interrupts` is separate and unchanged. It still gates delivery at the boundary. The shadow programs are cycle-identical. |
| HLT | The wake-up runs the same HARDWARE_IRQ path. `hlt_wakeup_intr_{rm,pm}` re-arm at `8E3`. |
| REP | An interrupted REP is delivered through the same path, so the re-arm is at the delivery tail, not at an iteration. `rep_stos_intr` and `rep_scas_intr_high_eip` re-arm at `8E3`, cycle-identical. |
| NMI | The NMI latch is untouched, and an NMI delivery runs no CINTLA. |
| fault during delivery (#NP/#GP/#PF on the IDT or stack) | The exception delivery ends with its own tail `i_rni`, after the second INTA. This is no change from the old rule for any PIC that drops INTR at the acknowledge. |
| V86 / EMM386 reflection | Ring 0 is entered through the `639` tail, which re-arms with IF=0. The monitor's reflection's own IRET/STI then behaves as before. The EMM and V86 fuzzers with `--irq` match (section 4). |

The one behavioural difference is the intended one. A PIC that keeps the acknowledged request's
INTR high past the delivery's end now gets a second acknowledge (a spurious IRQ7 on an 8259).
Section 2.3 bounds that window.

### 2.3 Is the PIC tolerance real at FULL speed?

I swept `+intr_drop_delay=D` (INTR held D platform cycles after the second INTA). I also added a
scratch variant, `hw_interrupt` with `STI` as the handler's first instruction. That is the shortest
route I could find to an IF=1 boundary in real mode.

The table gives the largest D that passes on `ebd1879`. `0b5b782` passes every D up to 200 in every
row.

| program | lat 0 | lat 7 | `+zero_wait` | `cpu_speed=1` | `cpu_speed=13` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `hw_interrupt` (IF clear, IRET) | 60 | 60 | 60 | 128 | >= 200 |
| `pm_trap_gate_if_preserve` (IF set at entry) | 64 | 72 | 64 | 128 | >= 200 |
| scratch: real mode, handler starts `STI` | **44** | 44 | 44 | 96 | >= 200 |

These confirm the author's 56-64 figures, with a lower floor of about 44 cycles for an
STI-first handler. Delivery is cache-resident, so `+zero_wait` does not shorten it.

The premise holds for this core. In `pc9821_inta_bridge.sv`, the second INTA puts the PIC's
acknowledge in S_INTA2 -> S_ACK, *before* the vector is returned in S_XFER, and
`cpu_intr <= pic_interrupt_do` is a single register. INTR therefore falls in the cycle of the
vector transfer, or one or two cycles after it. That is more than 40 cycles before the earliest
IF=1 boundary. The throttle only widens the margin. A real 8259 drops INT within the second INTA
pulse.

The D=32 guards sit about 12 cycles inside the tightest measured margin, so they are not flaky,
and the mutant in section 1 shows they bite.

- **Prefixes and headings.** The branch is `tests:` followed by `fix(interrupt_controller):`, each
  with Why / Upstream / Test / Fork. The red-until-fixed `tests:` commit is the house pattern, and
  its Test line says which programs fail before the fix and at which settings. I confirmed every
  cell of that claim (section 1).
- **Scope.** Commit 2 touches `interrupt_controller.sv` and the `docs/throttle.md` paragraph that
  explains the same rule. That is one intent in one commit.
- **The upstream claim is VERIFIED.**
  - Upstream `4bfdde0`'s `interrupt_controller.sv` has the identical
    `if (!intr) intr_latch_inhibit <= 1'b0;`, unchanged since `80562d4`. The defect is upstream's,
    and `fix(` is the correct prefix.
  - The hunk applies to `4bfdde0` with one line of context (offset -14). At the default three lines

  - `docs/throttle.md` is a fork-only file.

## 7. Answer

**Push: YES.** The re-arm at `i_rni` is the first boundary after the delivery. The monitor shows
this across every program and 20k fuzzed deliveries, on both delivery tails. It never takes an
interrupt a 486 would not. Its one new requirement is that the PIC drop the acknowledged INTR before
the first IF=1 boundary. That requirement has at least 44 platform cycles of margin at full speed,
against a PIC that drops it at the vector transfer. The fail-first holds at full speed and at 7, 9
and 13, and the guards bite a too-early re-arm. Cycles are identical across 1,005 program-latency
rows and 30 microbenchmark rows, the release gate is green, and the fuzzers with `--irq` and with
throttle variants match. I did not change the branch.
